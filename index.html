1. What the app is

The Daily Games is an invite-only challenge app for small groups of friends or family (maximum four people per challenge). There is no public feed, no user directory, and no advertising.

2. Information the app uses

Account. You sign in with Apple. The app stores the user identifier Apple provides. If you share your email with the app, it is only used to identify your account. You also choose a display name and optional bio, which other people in the same challenge can see.

Challenge data. Challenge name, dates, invite code, tasks, who joined or forfeited, and the daily numbers and notes you save. This lives on your device and is synced with Apple CloudKit so other members of that invite group can see the same challenge.

Health. If you add a Health-linked task and tap Sync, the app may read from Apple Health / Fitness on this device: steps, active energy, exercise minutes, activity ring totals used as task sources, and workout minutes. The app does not write to Health. Permission is optional; you can always type the number yourself. A value you save as part of a challenge may be synced to the invite group as a score. The app does not upload your full Health history.

Notifications. If you allow notifications, reminders are scheduled on your device while you are in an active challenge. No reminders between 22:00 and 08:00 local time. This version does not use a separate push server.

Diagnostics. Apple may collect crash data if you have agreed to that with Apple. The app does not include a separate advertising or analytics SDK.

3. What I do not do

  • Sell personal information
  • Use challenge or Health data for advertising
  • Run a public social network or user search
  • Require Health access to use the app

4. Who can see your data

Only people who join a challenge with its invite code can see that challenge’s tasks, logs, and display names. Sign in with Apple and CloudKit are operated by Apple under Apple’s policies. I do not run a separate company server that stores a second copy of your Health history.

5. How long data is kept

Data on your device stays until you delete the app or remove that challenge locally. CloudKit records remain so members can sync. Cancelled challenges disappear from the app lists; the underlying CloudKit rows may remain until they are deleted. You can stop using Sign in with Apple for this app in your Apple ID settings.

6. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13. Do not create an account for a child under 13.

7. Your choices

  • Refuse Health access in iOS Settings
  • Turn off notifications in iOS Settings
  • Edit your name and bio in Profile
  • Opt out of a forming challenge or forfeit an active one
  • Delete the app to remove local data

To ask that a specific CloudKit challenge be removed, email joshuapvr486@gmail.com with the invite code and your display name.

8. Changes

If this policy changes in a material way, the date at the top will be updated and the in-app Settings link will point here.
